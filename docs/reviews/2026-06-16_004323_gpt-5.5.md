## Overall

The core product thesis is strong: do not become another AI reviewer bot; become the comprehension layer that makes humans actually re-engage with PRs. The best part is the honesty: receipt, not approval; risk attributes, not verdicts; no general bug-hunt cosplay; delta brief as re-review memory reload.

My blunt take: the product is not fundamentally pointed the wrong way. But the current design is at risk of carrying too much old scope after the center-of-gravity shift. The strongest v1 is probably narrower than the docs still imply.

## What's Good

The re-review thesis is the clearest product wedge. The docs now say the lead value is re-review as a context-switch tax, and that is much sharper than "better code review" broadly.

The receipt model is excellent. "Tool issues receipt, not approval" is both ethically clean and commercially useful as an org audit trail.

The chunk/change-group/concern model is unusually well thought through. Intent living on the Change-group instead of every chunk fixes a real modeling bug. This is good product modeling, not just architecture.

The no-verdict Risk Surface stance is right. It avoids "AI says scary thing, reviewer obeys" anchoring, which the glossary correctly identifies as a chief enemy.

The delta design choices are strong: per-reviewer Checkpoint, tree diff rather than commit replay, and conservative flag carry-over. That is exactly the kind of correctness the product needs.

The revised Phase 1 plan is now aimed at the right question. Replacing the old Wizard-of-Oz Treatment/B' setup with real-tool dogfooding, status-quo GitHub as baseline, delta re-review as the primary test, and voluntary repeat usage as the kill criterion is a major improvement. It validates the new center of gravity instead of spending effort on the demoted playbook layer.

## Wrong Or Risky

The biggest risk is scope drift after the reshape. `CONTEXT.md` explicitly says Playbook is demoted and Mind-Map shrinks to persistence only. But older ADR/product language still gives them major conceptual weight. I would treat "private mind-map thinking space" as cut from the pitch unless proven again.

The author-side v1 expansion feels under-validated. The docs admit the evidence base is thin and author-side is the one scope expansion. ADR 0009 is coherent, but I would not let author-side compete with reviewer delta for v1 build gravity. Make it thin, read-only, and experimental.

The validation gate is now directionally correct, but looser. Dogfood plus repeat usage is more honest than a fake clinical test, but it still needs a concrete go/no-go floor before sessions: how many follow-up PRs, how many senior reviewers, what counts as visible behavior movement, and what level of voluntary reuse is enough to continue.

The updated validation plan may slightly overclaim categorizer maturity. "Built and gold-checked against Medusa" is good enough to dogfood, but not enough to declare broad categorizer accuracy across domains or change types. Treat it as "thin slice ready for behavior testing," not "Phase 2 solved."

The senior ICP tension is real. The strongest skeptic says playbooks may be noise for experienced reviewers, and the synthesis flags this as load-bearing. That does not kill the product, but it does mean human-facing playbooks should not be the senior workflow headline.

The standalone web app choice is defensible, but high stakes. ADR 0001 is right that GitHub's DOM is a bad home for this UX. Still, pulling reviewers out of GitHub means the experience must be obviously better within the first minute. "Nice structured diff" is not enough, especially with Linear nearby.

## Missing

A clearer "daily mode" vs "deep mode." The product says engagement costs time, but the actual UI/product contract needs to prevent every PR from feeling like homework. Delta, large PRs, auth/state/schema changes deserve ceremony. Tiny follow-ups need a very fast path.

One-line changes need special handling, but not exemption. LoC should affect UX friction and compute budget, not safety eligibility. A tiny PR can still be destructive: widening a permission check, changing a boolean condition, removing validation, changing a feature flag default, or bumping one dependency. The better rule is graduated response: tiny low-surface changes get a compact fast-lane brief, while tiny sharp changes escalate because their Risk Surface is high despite their size.

A sharper attention-routing answer. Risk Surface avoids verdicts, good. But users explicitly want to know "which parts matter." Flat factual tags may be too weak. You need a non-verdict way to route attention, maybe "because this touches auth and lacks tests" without saying "critical."

Security/trust posture is underdeveloped for an org product handling source code. The ADRs discuss storage mechanics, but less about secret redaction, data boundaries, model-provider exposure, BYOK/self-hosting posture, audit deletion, and least-privilege GitHub scopes. This will become a sales blocker earlier than expected.

The public Receipt may change reviewer behavior in weird ways. Dismissal reasons being public is honest, but it can also make reviewers defensive and generic. You need to validate whether people write useful reasons when they know authors/managers can inspect them.

The validation sample is still too friendly. The synthesis says the gate is not met and needs a non-adjacent stranger. Believe that warning.

## Recommendation

Make v1's center: **Delta Brief + Story/Risk Brief + Receipt**. Keep author self-review as a thin reuse of the same Brief, not a second product. Keep playbooks agent-facing or tucked away. Treat private mind-map as persistence, not positioning.

For validation, proceed with the revised dogfood Phase 1, but set the go/no-go floor before running it. The most important test is whether the Delta Brief changes re-review behavior on real follow-up PRs. If delta does not move that moment, separate a rendering failure from a concept failure before killing the wedge.

For activation and compute, avoid an all-or-nothing rule based on PR size. Run a cheap deterministic triage on every PR, then choose the experience: compact for low-surface changes, full Story/Risk or Delta treatment for sharp changes and returning-reviewer follow-ups.

The design is good because it keeps refusing seductive bad ideas. The danger is not a bad core thesis; it is building too much around older surfaces before the new thesis has been behaviorally proven.

## Disposition (2026-06-16)

**Actioned:**
- **Phase-1 hardening** (commit `0817163`): go/no-go floor committed before sessions (≥3 follow-up PRs, ≥3 seniors incl. ≥1 **non-adjacent**, reviewer engages ≥1 reopened item, ≥1 unprompted reuse); categorizer maturity softened to **N=1-calibrated** (not broad accuracy); non-adjacent reviewer required. (`phase-1.md`, `validation-plan.md`)
- **Graduated triage by Risk Surface, not size** → **ADR 0010** (+ `Triage`/`Fast-lane` glossary): deterministic floor → Haiku triage (escalate-on-doubt) → Opus on escalation; risk decides escalation, size decides spend; per-reviewer carry-over gate for follow-ups; Activation refined to triage-gated. Mid-review follow-ups handled via the **session baseline** (ADR 0006 #7).
- Already-aligned (no change needed): author-side kept thin/read-only/no-state (ADR 0009); playbook demoted + mind-map → persistence (CONTEXT / center-of-gravity).

**Deferred (tracked future work — not yet acted on):**
- **#5 Attention-routing via risk-surface *combinations*.** Flat factual tags may be too weak; surface the *combination* as the routing reason ("touches-auth + no-tests") without a "critical" verdict. Brief-presentation refinement; revisit when building the brief UI.
- **#6 Security / trust posture.** Secret redaction before the model, data boundaries, model-provider exposure, BYOK/self-host, audit deletion, least-privilege GitHub scopes. The review's "sales blocker earlier than expected" — the largest unaddressed gap. Needs its own ADR/doc before any org sale.
- **#7 Public-Receipt behavior + web-app first-minute bar.** Validate whether public dismissal reasons stay useful (don't go defensive/generic); ensure the standalone web app is obviously-better-in-a-minute. Both tied to surfaces not yet built.
