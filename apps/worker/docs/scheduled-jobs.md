# Scheduled jobs

- **Scheduled jobs are instance-wide and worker-only**: each runs on graphile's cron (its `*_CRONTAB` line joined into the runner's `crontab` in `main.ts`) and calls a persistence sweep that takes the handle, never a ctx, except the reconcile job below, which calls GitHub. Nothing on a request path may call one.
- `reconcile-webhook-deliveries` (every 15 minutes) reads the App's delivery log on its JWT and asks GitHub to resend what failed. It holds no state: `task/reconcile-webhook-deliveries/logic/select-redeliveries.ts` decides from the log alone, per `guid`, skipping any success, a 400 or 413, an original past 71 hours or out of view (its attempt count is unknown), a `github_app_authorization` (its delete has no confirmation, so a replay would end a later sign-in) and a `pull_request` or review its installation has a later removal for, dated by the removal's earliest attempt in view (a replay would recreate purged rows, and a retry's time is not when the removal happened), waiting 15 m, 1 h, 4 h then 12 h from the last attempt and stopping at 5, at most 20 a tick from at most 100 pages of the log (a failure older than the last page read is never seen). A read the cap stops then lists up to 10 pages of GitHub's failed-only log (`status=failure`, codes 400 to 599, so a never-connected attempt is not among them), so a tick lists at most 110 pages, 440 list requests an hour on the App JWT, and warns when a failure past the last page read, inside the 71 hours, is not a `check_run` or `status`, or when the pages read hold a never-connected attempt, since an older one past them is out of sight; otherwise it logs at info, a `check_run` or `status` (it only wakes a head to check it again) after every other due delivery. A failed list, a 422 or a rate limit warns and waits for the next tick; one delivery's refusal warns and moves on.
- `sweep-audit` (daily) calls `sweepAuditLog`; retention is an operator env setting. Unset retention deletes nothing; a window under the 30-day floor is refused.
- `sweep-sessions` (hourly) calls `sweepExpiredSessions`, then `sweepDeadCredentials` (dead grants, and tokens 30 days past revoke or expiry), always on: an expired session or dead token grants nothing. Each batch hands its orphaned grants over once committed, and the task revokes every one at GitHub in a `finally`, so a later failure still revokes what earlier batches removed. Revokes run three at a time inside a 30-second budget (`REVOKE_BUDGET_MS`) so a GitHub outage cannot hold the job slot; the rest are skipped with one warn naming the count, since the grants are gone and GitHub expires the tokens on its own. A failed revoke warns `grant.revoke.fail` and the sweep stands.
- `sweep-webhook-deliveries` (hourly) calls `sweepWebhookDeliveries`, always on: a delivery past the 72-hour window suppresses nothing.
- `sweep-generation-parts` (daily) calls `sweepStaleGenerationParts`, always on: a span whose newest part is over a week old goes whole, unless a live claim holds its head. A superseded, capped or failed run's parts have no other end short of the pull request's cascade.
- `sweep-pull-requests` (daily) calls `sweepClosedPullRequests`; retention is the instance config's `retention.pullRequestDays`, read by close time across both scope worlds. Unset retention deletes nothing; a window under one day is refused.
- **Failure dumps are swept per process, not by a queue job** (`failure-dump-sweep.ts`): at boot and once a day each worker deletes the `*.failed.json` files in its own dump folder (`FAILURE_DUMP_DIR` in `main.ts`) once they are seven days old, always on. The folder is the container's own disk, so a cron job, which one replica claims, would leave the other replicas' dumps in place; no retention setting or purge reaches it.
